EXB-LDR · LuckyDreamer Rhythm Board — installed in slot 1

A rhythm engine grafted from the LuckyDreamer artifact: 51 bell timelines and grooves, each carrying where it comes from and how much to trust the transcription (·t transcribed · ·d derived · ·? uncertain). Pick a figure, press start. The default voice is LDR PHYSICS — the donor's own percussion models (agogôs, congas, surdos, claves, timbales, shakers…), ported number-for-number, with every figure routed to the instrument it was written for; choose a TRITON kit instead for the classic lane sound. MELODIC mode makes the figure play whatever chord you hold on the keys (nothing held = it brings its own). ENSEMBLE adds the figure's documented companion parts. Tempo follows the unit's ♩ on the GLOBAL page. The big PLAY on the Lucky Dreamer 1U above uses this same board to conduct full band arrangements on its own.

Honesty note: every sound here is synthesized live in your browser with the Web Audio API, modeling the HI signal flow (oscillator → resonant filter → amp, with envelopes, LFOs, dual arpeggiator, insert and master effects, 62-voice cap and voice stealing). No Korg ROM samples or firmware are included — those belong to Korg. Panel behavior follows the TRITON-Rack basic manual's mode structure; the test-mode key combos mirror the ones documented in Triton-family service manuals.

This page is already the instrument — tap a patch and play, nothing to install. The zip below is strictly optional, for people who also want these sounds inside desktop music software: it renders the selected program into a pack for the free DecentSampler plugin, at your audio driver's latency.

One-tap sampler pack (.zip) Renders every key zone of the current program offline — computed sample-exact at 48 kHz / 24-bit, never recorded through speakers — writes loop points plus the program's chorus, delay and reverb into a .dspreset, and downloads a single zip. Pads and keys run 20–60 MB; the drum kit is small. Give it a minute on a phone.
