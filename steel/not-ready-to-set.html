Not Ready to Set

Every outfit on the job counts down to “before you set,” and you’re the one who finds out at six in the morning — with a crane, an operator and a raising gang already on the clock — which of them didn’t make it. Walk the pick before you call the iron up and name what stops the set, in your words: the anchor bolts that aren’t in or nobody’s shot, the embeds that never got cast, the deck below that isn’t poured, the crane that can’t get set up, the laydown that’s still full, the line over the swing nobody de-energized. Then send the only ask that matters — clear it and tell me when, or tell me in writing to fly it as it sits. Because a crane day doesn’t come back, and iron you set on somebody else’s condition is on your record.

What you send

It’s a condition list, not a verdict. Nothing on it says anybody’s anchor bolts, embeds or deck failed anything — it says what you found, where, in your words, with anything you checked taken off your own level and set beside what the approved erection set says. It states no torque, no bolt tension, no weld size, no capacity, no survey elevation and no threshold of any kind, and it never says a connection is ready to load or a bolt is right — that is the engineer’s call and the special inspector’s numbered record. It doesn’t stop the job: it hands whoever runs it the choice, on the record — clear it, or direct it set as it sits.

✦ A hold your crane mornings hit that isn’t on the list? Wish for it — it gets added, and you get the credit.
