Before We Close It

Run by run at quitting time: what’s open, what’s holding it, and the time the dirt goes back. One message before you’re off the machine.

Nothing on this page classes a soil, slopes, benches, shores or shields a trench, or says a trench is safe to enter or to work in. A run not named here is a run I have said nothing about.

The job — typed once, rides on every message

Three fields and no fourth. No “sent to”, because this one message goes to everybody at once — the EC, the plumber, the gas man, the low-voltage outfit, the surveyor and the super all need the same sentence.

Where each run got to

Name the run once — it carries, and it becomes a button. Then it’s pick where it got to, pick what’s holding it, put the time the dirt goes back, Add. Tap a row to move it one rung up the ladder — that is the whole end-of-day job: tap each run up to where it actually got.

The job tonight

Group by

The pencil opens a row to change what’s holding it, put the closing time on, flag it, or delete it. A row flagged Closing it in the morning or Nobody goes near it lands in its own block at the top of the message — those are the ones that get read first.

Send it — while the ditch is still open

Include

Still open is the message the electrician, the plumber and the gas man actually need — those are the runs they can still get into, and the time is how long they have. Held on somebody else is the super’s copy. Everything is tomorrow’s first move and your own record of the day. The spreadsheet copy is every row with a tab between the columns, and it’s also your backup: this lives in this browser on this phone.
