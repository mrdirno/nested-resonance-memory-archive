Give Me The Go

The slab reads wet. Or the heat has never run, the material never sat in the space, the joints were never filled, or the schedule says one thing and the plan hatches another — and the super says put it in, we hand over Friday. Once you spread glue that substrate is sealed forever and it is somebody else's ninety-day-old mistake that you now own. This is the last hour anybody can still see it. Write down what you're standing on, what you measured, what your own instructions require and what it costs you to sit — and end it the only way it ends: give me the go in writing, or tell me who's fixing it and by when.

What you send

This page never says ready, and it never says no. It sets no moisture limit, no flatness tolerance, no acclimation window and no temperature — it prints your reading beside the limit you typed off your own pail and lets the two of them sit next to each other, because those limits are your mill's and your adhesive maker's and they disagree with each other. Whether it can go down is your call with your instructions in your hand, and if the spec calls for an independent agency's test then that report is theirs, not this. If it's 9x9s, black mastic or anything pre-1978, stop — you don't say what it is on here and neither does anybody else until the owner's survey does.
