Doors · the cage full of cylinders nobody has decided about

Call The Keying

You’re holding forty-odd cylinders and nobody has called the keying — no meeting, no schedule, no word on whether you go on the construction key for now. Every day that stands, the second trip gets closer to being the plan. So send the ask, once, with a date on it: what’s in the cartons, what nobody’s told you, the day you have to put them in by — and what that day looks like without an answer, in your words. Either the direction comes down, or somebody says the word and the locks go on with temp cores so the building locks, and coming back to re-core is its own trip. It writes no groups, no master and no bitting, ever. It puts a date on somebody else’s decision.

What you send

It’s an ask, not a keying schedule. Nothing on it is a keyed-alike group, a master or grand-master structure, a bitting, a keyway or a cylinder-to-door crosswalk, and nothing on it asks for one — that schedule belongs to the owner, the hardware consultant and the distributor’s keying desk, and a second copy of it on a phone is the problem, not the fix. What’s here is what you are holding, what nobody has told you, and the day you have to put them in by. Going on the construction key for now is not a keying decision — a hung door has to lock — and this page never says the owner is fine with it: what that day looks like is yours to write, in your words, so the choice is theirs, on the record. Photograph the cartons and the tags, never a key — a clear photo of a key is its bitting. The doorway version of this — the opening that isn’t ready because the keying isn’t called — is a stop on Not Ready To Hang; who takes the cores at the end of an occupied night is Getting In; the turnover you write when they finally do come out is Write-Up Setup.
