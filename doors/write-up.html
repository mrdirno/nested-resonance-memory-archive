Doors · the paperwork you put off

The Write-Up Setup

The frames that were set before you got there, out of plumb and grouted solid by the time you found them. The opening you flagged and were told to hang anyway. The truck that came up four frames short while the driver was already pulling the gate down. What your number didn’t cover. The day your crew stood in a corridor. Your own walk through the openings before the architect runs his — and whatever else you have to write that isn’t on the list. Pick the one you’re stuck with and this hands you the exact instructions to paste into your AI — once. After that it’s: dump the mess at the tailgate, get back something you can send without touching it. It never states a label, a clearance, a closer setting or a keying group, and it never calls an opening complete — it writes down what you saw, what your tape read and what you did.

Nothing you type here leaves your phone — it stays in this browser. What you paste into your AI afterwards is your call, so keep customer detail and anyone’s personal information out of it. This builds instructions, not certified data: it never supplies a fire rating or says what a label permits, never states a clearance, gap, undercut, closer setting or opening force, never reproduces a hardware set or a door schedule, and never carries keying of any kind — and it never states that an opening was complete, compliant or ready for anybody’s inspection. Check anything feeding a purchase, a pay application or a safety decision against your own approved submittal, the shop drawing in your hand, and the people certified to make those calls.
