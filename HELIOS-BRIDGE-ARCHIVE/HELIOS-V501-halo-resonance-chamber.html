Backreaction. Every other force here is prescribed — the eigenmode is an exact solution and the particles ride it. Turn this up and the swarm deposits its own density on a 32³ mesh, a Poisson solve turns that into a potential, and its gradient goes back into the force: matter starts writing the field it moves in. The source is the density contrast, so a smooth swarm feels nothing and only structure pulls — the same particle-mesh scheme, and the same Jeans swindle, that cosmological N-body codes use. It crosses a threshold: inside Spinning Chladni 0.3 leaves the swarm diffuse and 0.8 pulls it into one dense core. That looked like gravity against the expansion; measured, it is not — a factor of eight in the expansion rate moves the threshold by eight percent. What holds the ball up is its own spin: the helix drive turns the swarm at 6 × helix / damping radians per second, and centrifugal balance predicts the threshold within ten percent at helix 0.8 and 1.2 (on this page: holds at 0.45 and folds at 0.6 at helix 0.8, holds at 0.55 and folds at 0.75 at helix 1.2). Turn the helix down and the matter goes to the poles before gravity acts; what gravity then makes there are polar clumps, at gains down to a floor near 0.2–0.3 that neither the spin nor the field nor the expansion sets. The core that forms is a numerical object: its size is the per-step kick of the 500 force ceiling, and more substeps shrink it toward the mesh cell. Costs roughly one extra particle pass per tick.

Self-gravity solver

Six sweeps is what every preset was found with. Each tick the potential is nudged six times toward the answer, starting from last tick’s potential. Small features settle in a fraction of a second. The broadest part — the pull of the whole swarm on itself — settles with a time constant near two seconds, so it runs late: on a running Spinning Chladni swarm three seconds in, the six-sweep potential was a third off the exact one. Exact solves the same equation on the same mesh to the end, every tick, so the long-wavelength part of the potential is no longer late. On a frozen swarm, once the sweeps have caught up, the two agree to about two parts in a million. What changes is how quickly a large mass feels its own weight, not how hard. It costs about the same: within a millisecond a tick at four million particles.

Self-gravity mesh

Nearest cell is what every preset was found with. A particle drops its whole mass into the one mesh cell it is in and reads the pull at that cell, so the pull jumps when a particle crosses a cell edge (cells are 0.96 wide here). Cloud-in-cell shares each particle’s mass among its eight nearest cells by distance and reads the pull back the same way, so forces change smoothly across the mesh and a particle feels no pull from its own mass. It writes eight cells per particle instead of one, and that is the cost. On the author’s Apple M4 Pro one tick at a million particles took 4 ms with nearest cell and 24 ms with cloud-in-cell; at 4.2 million, 12 ms and 105 ms.

Gain / loss off

An open cavity. Damping alone drains energy everywhere at one rate and leaves the mode structure untouched; this makes the drain positional — the current mode’s own antinode lobes are pumped while the space between them is drained, in balance. Matter then circulates from the lossy regions into the amplifying ones and a driven steady state replaces the decaying one, which is what separates a non-Hermitian cavity from a lossy Hermitian one. The pattern re-locks to every new mode, so the pumping map changes with the digits.

A perfectly symmetric Hubble flow cannot twist — it is irrotational. Anisotropy stretches the expansion along one axis (Zel'dovich-style), and helical shear adds the torque; together they wind the outflow into helical wakes.

Every figure has a twin: the same shape moved a quarter of a lobe around the axis. The two share one energy, so the cavity treats them as one pair. This switch couples them. κ is how fast the pair trades its content, γ feeds partner 1 and drains partner 2 at the same rate, Δ pushes their pitches apart. At γ = κ with Δ = 0 the pair reaches its exceptional point: its two natural motions merge into one. The loop takes γ and Δ once around that point every 20 seconds, starting and ending with no gain. Go clockwise and the pair ends mostly on partner 1; go counter-clockwise and it ends mostly on partner 2, whichever way it started. Flat sweeps Δ with no gain and brings the pair back where it began. Wells form: the particles feel the still part of the pair. Chladni form: they feel the intensity of the pair. Off, nothing here touches the field. Benettin’s method: about a thousand particle pairs start 0.001 apart and ride the full dynamics; every half second their separation is logged and squeezed back to 0.001 along the direction it grew. λ is the mean growth rate per second of chamber time. Positive means neighbouring paths diverge — chaos, with 1/λ the time to lose one e-fold of predictability. Negative means the swarm is contracting onto the figure. Pairs split by a wall or a wrap are re-paired, not scored, and pairs touching the reflecting wall are renormalized but not scored either: the wall’s kick alone reads about +0.4/s on a swarm that is otherwise neutral, and the meter should report the flow, not the wall. On ceiling is the share of the sampled particles whose total force hit the 500 ceiling this tick; near 100% the ceiling, not the model, is setting the speed.

Liouville: a frictionless, exactly rotated swarm keeps its phase-space volume (rate 0); damping shrinks it at three times the damping rate; the explicit magnetic kick inflates it. Measured beside predicted. The meter follows 64 small clusters. Each is one particle and six neighbours placed 0.001 away: three along the position axes, three along the velocity axes, so together they mark out a tiny box in the six numbers that describe a particle. Every half second the box is read back, its volume compared with the starting volume, and the box squared up and shrunk back to 0.001, the same reset the Lyapunov pairs get. Predicted comes from the step itself: damping at rate γ shrinks the box at 3γ per second; the Euler magnetic step also multiplies it by 1 + θ² on every substep, with θ the turn per substep (coupling × 10.5 × dt), so it adds ln(1 + θ²) / dt; the exact rotation adds nothing. With gain/loss on, the damping differs from place to place and the prediction reads “local” instead of a number. Clusters touching the wall at the end of the half second, on the force ceiling at either end of it, or stretched past 0.1 — a hundred times their size, where the force across them is no longer one straight line, and a wrap may have split them — are reset and not scored, and the half second around a zoom-out is skipped, as for the pairs. What a mismatch means: with a full scored count and nothing on the ceiling, measured and predicted should agree within a few percent. A gap means something the formula does not know about acted inside the half second, most often a wall bounce that ended away from the wall. A scored count near zero means the swarm is fast and near the wall, or the graphics card stores positions too coarsely to hold a 0.001 offset. Substeps is the count that ran.

Turn the instruments on to read the energy.

H adds up, over every particle, its kinetic energy, the field’s energy at its position, and its share of the swarm’s own gravity. The field energy is worked out from the force, so that the force is the downhill slope of that energy; the gravity share is half the mesh potential at the particle’s cell, less the mean-density term the mesh subtracts. The stored velocity sits half a step behind the position (that is how the step works), so H is shown twice: at the stored velocity, and centred, with half a step of the force added, at the position’s own time. The band the stored one swings in is the integrator’s bookkeeping, not a leak; the centred one is the number to watch. H0 is H when the instruments came on or the settings last changed. It restarts at every digit change, zoom-out and reseed. With the six-sweep solver it waits until the mesh potential has settled; Potential settled says how well the potential fits the matter right now. With damping, expansion, helix, gain/loss, centers, vessel, overlays and the magnetic term off, and the field held still, the centred H should stay in a narrow band that does not walk. The wall is the one thing that is not part of H: a particle that hits it keeps half of its speed into the wall. With the wall ledger on, the page replays each step’s own velocity program with the wall switched off, sees exactly what every hit took, and adds it back: ΔH / H0 then includes what the wall took, and should hold. The ledger costs about as much as the physics itself while it is on. Rate is the energy change per second per unit of kinetic energy over the last second, beside what the settings predict. Damping takes twice its rate. The Euler magnetic step adds energy at a rate set by the size of one step’s turn and the share of motion across the field lines, measured, not assumed. The exact-rotation step adds nothing but what the graphics card’s own cosine and sine lose, which the page measures too. A gap between measured and predicted means something not on that list is doing work: the force ceiling (On ceiling, all counts every particle), or the mesh force. The mesh force is the potential’s slope at the particle’s cell, not exactly the downhill direction of the energy written here; that gap is the scheme’s own error, and the number shown is its size. L is the total angular momentum, L lined up its size as a share of what it would be if every particle circled the same way. The field is not round, so L is not expected to hold while the field is on. The wall pushes straight back along the radius and leaves L alone; the wrap flips the contribution of every particle it moves. Under the swarm’s gravity alone L should nearly hold, and the cube-shaped mesh is what breaks it. Field force off removes the field force without moving the strength slider, for the gravity audit. Both switches are saved with the rest of the settings.

Matter on partner 1 reads the same share off the matter itself, with the instruments on. Resonance Chamber

A GPU particle simulation of standing waves in a spherical cavity, with the wave's mode numbers sequenced by the digits of mathematical constants — and a small evolving universe of collapsing, merging, detonating centers living inside the field.

The field

Ψ(r,θ,φ) = jₗ(zₗₙ·r/R) · Pₗᵐ(cos θ) · cos(mφ − ωt)

The exact eigenmodes of a spherical cavity of radius R: spherical Bessel functions jₗ set the radial shells (zₗₙ is the n-th zero, so Ψ vanishes on the wall), associated Legendre functions Pₗᵐ set the latitude bands, and cos(mφ) the azimuthal lobes. The quantum numbers (n, l, m) are the same ones that label electron orbitals and the acoustic oscillation modes of stars. Mode energy is z²ₗₙ in natural units. A slow azimuthal precession ω (optional) makes the pattern rotate — twist without breaking the eigenmode. The radial profiles are computed on the CPU with a stable downward recurrence (validated against arbitrary-precision references to ~10⁻¹⁵) and streamed to the GPU as a lookup table; the angular parts are evaluated per particle in the shader.

Chladni mode — structure from absence

S = jₗ·(P̃ₗᵐ·cos(mφ̃) − P̃ₗᵐ²·sin(m₂φ̃)),  a = −A·∇S²

On a vibrating plate, sand collects where the amplitude is zero — the pattern is drawn by the places the wave is absent. Here two degenerate azimuthal orders (m and l−m) of the same (n, l) shell are superposed; S is still an exact eigenmode, and descending ∇S² drives every particle onto the curved 2-D surface S = 0 — the spherical Chladni figure, the locus of total destructive interference. Each digit step selects a new mode and the nodal surface reshapes.

Cosmos

Expansion: an outward acceleration proportional to radius which, against damping, produces a Hubble-like flow v = H·r — collapse and expansion compete, as they do in structure formation. Epochs: at fixed intervals the frame rescales by ½ (a comoving zoom-out). The field equations are identical in every epoch; only the inherited structure differs — relics of earlier epochs persist as compact knots while the same physics organizes the next, larger scale. Magnetism: a background axial field plus a dipole on each collapse center (moments flip with the pole); the Lorentz force v × B twists inflows and ejecta without doing work. Topology: the wall either reflects, or — in no-edge mode — identifies antipodal points, the construction that makes elliptic space finite yet edgeless.

Collapse centers

a = −w·G·r/(|r|²+ε²)^(3/2) → detonation → shell: a = A(R)·exp(−(|r|−R)²/σ²)·r̂

A feedback cycle borrowed from structure formation. Each center is a softened point attractor (a Plummer potential) with a mild tangential term, so matter spirals in as it accretes. Its mass — drawn from the digit stream at spawn, like its position — sets both its strength and its lifespan: heavier centers age longer. At the end of life the pole flips: attraction reverses and a traveling spherical blast shell launches, expanding at constant speed with amplitude decaying as it spreads. Shells from one detonation sweep through the territory of other centers, so the standing eigenmode field, the collapses, and the traveling waves feed back on one another. On screen each center is drawn as a pure black occluding sphere — a silhouette against its own accretion disc, the way a black hole is seen only by what it gathers. And the walls answer: in reflect mode each shell returns as an echo from its acoustic image source outside the sphere; in no-edge mode the wavefront refocuses near the antipode of its origin and re-expands, as waves do in a closed space — two echo generations at 65% strength, then silence. Growth is modeled by age rather than by measured accretion, and the centers are external potentials, not self-gravitating masses.

Lineage — heredity across scales

Structure formation is hierarchical: when two collapsed objects meet they don't erase each other — they merge, and the remnant carries the progenitors' cores inside it as substructure. Here that becomes heredity. Centers drift under mutual gravity; when two coalesce, the heavier absorbs the lighter, heritable traits (swirl handedness, magnetic polarity) cross over digit-deterministically, the lineage generation climbs, the old core stays visibly embedded in the remnant, and the coalescence radiates a burst — the chirp, as merging compact objects do. At every epoch zoom-out the heaviest survivor seeds a mirrored daughter at its antipode with a digit-mutated genome: selection, replication, variation — one generation per epoch. A passing blast front can also flip an accreting center's swirl and shock it toward earlier collapse, the analogue of shock-triggered star formation. Nothing here uses a random generator: every trait, merger outcome, and mutation is a function of the digit stream.

Sound — the SoundHack bridge

The cavity modes are acoustic modes, so the chamber can play itself (speaker button, or A). Two voices, chosen in the Sound panel. Digit steps plays each step's eigenfrequency once — pitch follows zₗₙ, a soft fifth above. SoundHack bridge (default) keeps six oscillators running at the cavity's own overtone series — the ratios zₗ,ₖ/zₗ,ₙ of the current degree l — and at every digit transition morphs the sounding spectrum from the outgoing mode to the incoming one using Larry Polansky's morphological mutation functions, the spectral-mutation engine of Tom Erbe's SoundHack (1991), ported from the archived C. Both blended states sound at once during a morph, so their beat frequencies are real, and the mutation index Ω follows the digit stream by default — the no-random-generator contract extends into the audio. A Tuning control transposes both voices by up to five octaves down (a cavity this size would really oscillate far below hearing, so the register is the listener's concession, not the chamber's). Detonations still land a thump; mergers still chirp.

Vessels, sculpture, and export

The chamber can pour the same physics into other molds. A vessel (Overlays panel) confines the swarm to a surface — a ring, a vase, a flat panel (a true 2-D Chladni plate), or a spherical shell — and the eigenmode field then patterns the matter on that surface: the same brush on a new canvas. Pause to freeze a sculpture, then Export point cloud writes the live particle positions (with speeds) as CSV through the viewer's save prompt — open it in CloudCompare or MeshLab, compute normals, run Poisson reconstruction, and you have a meshable, sliceable, printable object grown from standing waves. Your presets (Simulation panel) capture everything — physics, sequence, overlays, vessel, cosmos, sound, colors, particle count, camera, and the exact step — so any state you find can be recalled or shared as text.

Lab — the chamber measuring itself

Four instruments (rail tab 7) read the GPU state back once a second and report numbers about the dynamics rather than pictures of it. Lyapunov exponent: Benettin’s twin-particle method — pairs started 0.001 apart, separation logged every half second and renormalized along its own direction — gives the mean rate at which neighbouring paths diverge; positive is chaos, negative is contraction onto the figure. Phase-space volume: small seven-particle clusters mark out a box in position and velocity, and the rate at which that box grows or shrinks is measured and shown beside what the step predicts — zero for a frictionless exact rotation, minus three times the damping, plus the inflation of an explicit magnetic step. Memory across epochs: at every zoom-out the ending density is kept as a relic map, and M is the cell-by-cell correlation between the matter now and where the relics predict it; the value just before the next zoom-out is the memory the scale retained, and the same correlation against the relic from two zoom-outs ago is reported beside it as the control — a script that recurs, or a blob that persists, scores on both, and only an epoch imprinting the next lifts one above the other. This is the project’s own claim, nested resonance memory, made into a number with its null built in. Realized spectrum: a real spherical-harmonic transform of the density mesh gives the angular power the matter actually carries, against the degree the digits asked for. An Integrator control (Simulation panel) adds substeps, or picks the count itself under Auto, because the magnetic term at high coupling turns a velocity by more than a radian per clamped step and the instruments should describe the model, not the step; the meter also reports the share of particles on the force ceiling, which is the quickest way to tell a state of the model from a state of the integrator.

The dynamics

a = −A·∇Ψ,  v ← (v + a·dt)·e^(−γ·dt),  x ← x + v·dt

Each particle accelerates down the gradient of Ψ, so the swarm collects along the field's nodal structure. The gradient is evaluated analytically (not by finite differences), and integration is semi-implicit Euler with exponential damping. The physics advances on a fixed tick of 1/20 s on every machine and the renderer interpolates between ticks, so a preset means the same thing at 30 Hz and at 120 Hz, and every number in the rings below was measured at that tick. The magnetic term can be stepped two ways (Simulation panel): added as a force, which is how every preset was found and which pumps energy at 55 × coupling² × dt per second, or as an exact Boris rotation under which it does no work. All of it runs on the GPU: positions and velocities live in floating-point textures updated by fragment shaders, which is what makes millions of particles (up to 4.19M) interactive.

The sequence

Every step, each axis reads the next digit of its assigned constant — π and e (transcendental), √2 and φ (algebraic irrationals) — from 2,500 verified decimal digits. The digit stream acts as a deterministic, statistically uniform mode sequencer: the structure you see is physics; the schedule is arithmetic. Axis offsets (prime-number strides by default) decorrelate the three streams. In bases other than ten, the fractional parts are re-expanded exactly with big-integer arithmetic and digits are rescaled to a common mode range.

The mode dimer

Every figure has a twin: the same figure a quarter phase on (cos becomes sin in each term). The two share one energy, so the chamber can run them as one coupled pair with gain on one and loss on the other (Sequence panel). The pair's two amplitudes are integrated on the CPU each tick and their mix sets the field the particles feel. At gain equal to coupling with no detuning the pair sits at its exceptional point, where its two natural motions merge into one. Driving the pair once around that point sends it to one partner or the other, depending on the direction: clockwise mostly partner 1, counter-clockwise mostly partner 2. The same drive with no gain brings it back. The Lab panel shows the pair's rates, the shares, and what the matter itself reads.
