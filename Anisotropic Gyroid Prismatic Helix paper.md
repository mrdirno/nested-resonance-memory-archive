# Anisotropic Gyroid Prismatic Helix as a Unified Model of Expansion, Biological Load Paths, and Rotational Segmentation
**Author:** Aldrin Payopay  
**Date:** 2025  

## ABSTRACT
This paper defines a three-component geometric system that unifies cosmological expansion mapping, biological structural anisotropy, and rotational articulation: the Anisotropic Gyroid Prismatic Helix (AGPH). The model uses: (1) a prismatic macro-domain with axial scale factor a(z), (2) an anisotropically deformed gyroid lattice with tensor A(z), and (3) a cumulative helical twist field θ(z). The resulting structure reproduces the essential geometric behaviors of limb evolution (tapering, differential stiffness, torsion), bone trabeculation, photonic gyroid microstructures, and directional cosmological redshift scaling. AGPH provides a unified generative template for functional art objects, structural composites, and conceptual cosmology visualizations.

## 1. INTRODUCTION
Standard gyroid structures are defined inside cubic domains and treated as mechanically isotropic or directionally tuned through uniform deformation. This restricts their expressive and physical accuracy. Biological structures—bones, tendons, vascular geometries—do not follow cubic frames. Cosmological expansion is likewise non-Cartesian when expressed as scale factor a(z). The AGPH framework replaces the cube with a tapered prism, embeds an anisotropic gyroid field inside it, and applies a helical rotation along the longitudinal axis. This produces a geometry that simultaneously matches natural load-path tapering, anisotropic internal structure, and joint-based rotational progression.

## 2. MACRO-DOMAIN: PRISMATIC SCALE FACTOR
Let the macro shape be a prism P defined along axis z. The cross-sectional scale factor a(z) defines progressive widening or narrowing.  
P(z) = a(z) * S, where S is the base cross-section.  
This replaces cubic tiling with tapered or expanding domains, matching biological limb taper, cosmological scale factor behavior, and mechanical gradient requirements.

## 3. INTERNAL FIELD: ANISOTROPIC GYROID
Gyroid level-set equation:  
G(x, y, z) = sin(x)cos(y) + sin(y)cos(z) + sin(z)cos(x) = t.  

Anisotropy via deformation tensor A(z):  
(x’, y’, z’) = A(z) · (x, y, z).  

This matches trabecular bone anisotropy, insect wing photonic gyroids, and engineered metamaterials.

## 4. TORSIONAL FIELD: HELICAL ROTATION
Define cumulative twist field θ(z).  
R(z) = rotation_z(θ(z)).  
(x”, y”, z”) = R(z) · (x’, y’, z’).  

Models joint torsion: forearm pronation, humeral torsion, phalanx rotation.

## 5. UNIFIED MODEL: AGPH
AGPH(x, y, z) = G(A(z) · R(z) · (x, y, z * a(z))).  

Produces prismatic taper + anisotropy + torsion in one generative system.
