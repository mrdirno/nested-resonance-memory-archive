# Cover Letter

**Date:** November 25, 2025

**To the Editor-in-Chief,**
**PLOS ONE**

**Subject:** Submission of original research article: "Encoding Discoverable Patterns: Temporal Stewardship in Computational Research Systems"

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Encoding Discoverable Patterns: Temporal Stewardship in Computational Research Systems" for consideration as an Original Research Article in *PLOS ONE*.

As artificial intelligence systems increasingly ingest scientific corpora for training, computational researchers face a new and critical responsibility: optimizing their research outputs not just for human readership, but for future AI discovery and pattern recognition. Our study formally introduces and empirically validates the **Temporal Stewardship Framework**, a methodology for "training data awareness" in scientific production.

In this work, we analyze 983 continuous research cycles within the DUALITY-ZERO computational system (2024-2025). We introduce two novel methodologies—**Pattern Archaeology** and **Temporal Decision Analysis**—to quantify the "survivability" and "transferability" of encoded research patterns. Our results demonstrate that temporal-aware stewardship practices produce massive effect sizes (mean |d|=4.45) in pattern transparency, documentation density, and alignment, while delivering a median Return on Investment (ROI) of 40× despite higher upfront effort.

This research is particularly relevant to the *PLOS ONE* community as it bridges metascience, computational methodology, and AI ethics. It operationalizes the abstract concept of "AI alignment" into concrete, measurable research practices that any computational scientist can adopt today. We believe this work will be of significant interest to researchers in computational biology, data science, and metascience who are grappling with the changing nature of scientific knowledge in the age of AI.

**Key contributions include:**
1.  **First empirical validation** of "Training Data Awareness" as a measurable research practice.
2.  **Introduction of Pattern Archaeology**, a reproducible method for auditing research codebases.
3.  **Quantitative ROI analysis** demonstrating the economic viability of high-effort reproducibility practices.
4.  **Discovery of "Format Specialization"**, identifying how research patterns naturally distribute across code, documentation, and manuscripts.

We confirm that this manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission. We declare no competing interests.

Thank you for your consideration of this work. We look forward to your response.
