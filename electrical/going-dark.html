Electrical · the shutdown notice, before you throw it

Going Dark

You’re killing power to a building somebody else runs — a panel, a floor, the whole service — for a window you set and they have to live through. Everybody in it finds out one of two ways: from this notice, or when the elevator stops. Write it down once: what goes dark and where, the window in your words, every system that rides on it and whose call it is, what you need from the building to make the hour, and who answers the phone at both ends. Then send it, and don’t throw it until the go comes back.

What you send

It’s a notice, not a lockout and not a permit. It tells the building what’s going off, when, and what rides on it — in your words, off your own directory and one-line. It states no load, no ampacity, no clearance, no arc-flash boundary and no procedure; it never says a system is safe to shut down, safe to restart, or that a battery or a UPS will carry anything for any length of time. Lockout/tagout is on your program and your locks. The energized-work permit, the switching order and the utility’s side are theirs to issue, theirs to number and theirs to run — this page never stands in for any of them.

✦ A system the building always asks about after the lights go off that isn’t on the list? Wish for it — it gets added, and you get the credit.
