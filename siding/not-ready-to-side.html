Siding · at the wall, before the crew climbs

Wall’s Not Ready

You work between dry-in and paint on the outside of a building, and you are the one who finds out at seven in the morning what didn’t get done behind you. Walk the elevation before the crew climbs and name what stops the wall, in your words: the wrap that isn’t on or is torn, the windows nobody flashed, the sheathing with gaps in it, nothing to nail a corner to, the rot you found when the old wall came off, the colour nobody’s picked. Then send the only ask that matters — fix it and tell me when, or tell me in writing to close it as it sits. Because once the wall is on, whatever is behind it is behind it, and the man who closed it owns how it looks from the street.

What you send

It’s a condition list, not a verdict. Nothing on it says anybody’s work failed anything — it says what you found, where, in your words, off your own tape and set beside what your own set says. It states no fastener, no spacing, no gap, no clearance to grade, to a roof or to a deck, no gutter size and no threshold of any kind; it never says a wall is ready, weathertight or complete; and it never says what caused what you found, what it needs, or whether anything on an old wall is safe to cut — those are an engineer’s, an inspector’s and a certified outfit’s calls and this page stands in for none of them. It doesn’t stop the job either: it hands whoever runs the job the choice, on the record: clear it, or direct it closed as it sits.

✦ A hold your mornings hit that isn’t on the list? Wish for it — it gets added, and you get the credit.
