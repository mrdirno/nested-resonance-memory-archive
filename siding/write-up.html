The Write-Up Setup

What was behind the old wall the day it came off — the soft sheathing, the window nobody flashed, the wrap that was never there — against what you were told. The elevation you were handed, out of plane with nothing to nail a corner to, and who you told before the crew climbed. The day you couldn’t hang, with the wind on the gable and a crew standing, and who called it. The walk that came back about the wall and not the siding. The hole somebody cut through your finished elevation, and who cut it. Whose joint it is where your trim meets the window. The house you handed back and what you told them, in your own words — and whatever else you have to write that isn’t on the list. Pick the one you’re stuck with and this hands you the exact instructions to paste into your AI — once. After that it’s: dump the mess at the truck, get back something you can send without touching it. It never states a fastener, a gap, a clearance, a gutter size or a cause, and it never calls a wall flat or a job accepted — it writes down what you saw, what you were handed, what you did and who you told.

Nothing you type here leaves your phone — it stays in this browser. What you paste into your AI afterwards is your call, so keep customer detail and anyone’s personal information out of it. This builds instructions, not certified data: it never supplies a fastener, a spacing, a gap, an overlap, an exposure or a clearance — the manufacturer’s printed instructions own those, cited as an address; never sizes a gutter or a downspout — that is a published table and somebody’s calculation; never says a surface is or is not lead, asbestos, rot, mould or sound — the certified firm, the engineer and the inspector own those calls; never says why it happened or who caused it; and it never states that a wall is closed, weathertight, warrantable or accepted. Check anything feeding a purchase, a pay application or a safety decision against your own contract, the instructions in your hand, and the people certified to make those calls.
