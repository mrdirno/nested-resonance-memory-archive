Through My Wall

Twelve kits on this rack count down to a wall closing and every one of them means the inside face — because that’s where the rock hangs and the inspector looks. The outside face closes too. It closes once. And every hole anybody still needs after that is a hole saw through finished siding, a patch and a caulk joint the owner can see from the driveway — and from the street it looks like your work. So walk the elevation before you close it and put a row on every penetration somebody else needs: whose it is, what it is, where off a corner you can point at, whether it’s marked, through, or through with flashing on it, and the gate it has to beat. Send it to each outfit while it’s still a drill.

What you were told and what you saw, not a detail. Every row is a hole somebody else needs through your wall, in your words, off your own walk and the set named at the top. This page supplies no hole size, no spacing, no clearance to anything, no flashing detail and no sealant — the outfit that owns the penetration and the printed instructions you were handed own all of that. “There’s flashing on it” means you looked at it and photographed it; it is never a call that the flashing is right, and nothing on this page says a wall or a penetration is weathertight, correct or complete. It’s an ask to close out what has to come through before you hang: not an RFI, not a change order, and it doesn’t authorize anybody to do extra work. Double-check it before you send it, because a hole nobody walked is a cut somebody pays for.

The job — typed once, rides on every copy

Name the set and its revision, and put a name on every row. A line set counted against what the mechanical foreman told you on Tuesday is something he can confirm from his truck this afternoon; the same line set described from memory is a phone call in November, after the wall is on. And whatever somebody told you at the tailgate, put it in a row with their name on it.

Each hole through it

Name the spot once — it becomes a button and it carries. The elevation and the gate carry to the next row too, because you walk one face at a time. Leave anything blank you didn’t see: an empty field is honest and a guessed one is not.
