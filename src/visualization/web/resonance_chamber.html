Resonance Chamber

A GPU particle simulation of standing waves in a cubic cavity, with the wave's mode numbers sequenced by the digits of mathematical constants.

The field

Ψ(r) = cos(m·k·x + φₘ) · cos(n·k·y + φₙ) · cos(p·k·z + φₚ),  k = π/L

A separable three-dimensional standing wave in a box of half-width L. The integers m, n, p set how many spatial oscillations fit along each axis — the same mode numbers that label eigenstates of a particle in a box or the nodal patterns of a Chladni plate, extended to a volume. Each phase is tied to its digit, φ = (d/9)·π.

Chladni mode — structure from absence

S(r) = ψ(m,n,p) − ψ(p,m,n) + ψ(n,p,m),  a = −A·∇S²

On a vibrating plate, sand collects where the amplitude is zero — the pattern is drawn by the places the wave is absent. This mode reproduces that physics in a volume: the three cyclic permutations of (m, n, p) are degenerate eigenmodes (they share the energy m²+n²+p²), so their superposition S is itself an exact eigenmode, and descending the gradient of S² drives every particle onto the curved 2-D surface where S = 0 — the three-dimensional Chladni figure, the locus of total destructive interference. Each digit step selects a new degenerate family and the nodal surface reshapes.

Collapse centers

a = −w·G·r/(|r|²+ε²)^(3/2) → detonation → shell: a = A(R)·exp(−(|r|−R)²/σ²)·r̂

A feedback cycle borrowed from structure formation. Each center is a softened point attractor (a Plummer potential) with a mild tangential term, so matter spirals in as it accretes. Its mass — drawn from the digit stream at spawn, like its position — sets both its strength and its lifespan: heavier centers age longer. At the end of life the pole flips: attraction reverses and a traveling spherical blast shell launches, expanding at constant speed with amplitude decaying as it spreads. Shells from one detonation sweep through the territory of other centers, so the standing eigenmode field, the collapses, and the traveling waves feed back on one another. Growth is modeled by age rather than by measured accretion, and the centers are external potentials, not self-gravitating masses.

The dynamics

a = −A·∇Ψ,  v ← (v + a·dt)·e^(−γ·dt),  x ← x + v·dt

Each particle accelerates down the gradient of Ψ, so the swarm collects along the field's nodal structure. The gradient is evaluated analytically (not by finite differences), and integration is semi-implicit Euler with exponential damping, independent of frame rate. All of it runs on the GPU: positions and velocities live in floating-point textures updated by fragment shaders, which is what makes a million particles interactive.

The sequence

Every step, each axis reads the next digit of its assigned constant — π and e (transcendental), √2 and φ (algebraic irrationals) — from 2,500 verified decimal digits. The digit stream acts as a deterministic, statistically uniform mode sequencer: the structure you see is physics; the schedule is arithmetic. Axis offsets (prime-number strides by default) decorrelate the three streams. In bases other than ten, the fractional parts are re-expanded exactly with big-integer arithmetic and digits are rescaled to a common mode range.
