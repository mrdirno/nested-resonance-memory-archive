## Result

The replacement estimator was run once, under the frozen protocol, on the recorded grid. The result is **insufficient support**: the estimator declares **0 of 60 runs measurable**, and the protocol needs three conditions with all three seeds measurable before any of its recovery or robustness verdicts mean anything.

| Falsifier | Verdict | What it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| F1 identity checks | pass | every recorded mesh replaced by its orbit means: 0 eligible epochs; a static Plummer sphere: 0 |
| F2 false positives | pass | the synthetic receipt only: nine controls at or below the upper 95 % binomial bound for a 5 % rate and two AR(1) controls at size 8.9 % against a 10 % ceiling. Two of the field controls are thin: the four-fold noise-spread control has no measurable run (the seed-balance gate removes all 90, so it passes by exclusion) and the three-fold one has 12 of 90 measurable with 1 detection against a bound of 2; and the sampling interval of the 8.9 % size reaches the ceiling |
| F3 recovery | not evaluable | no measurable run to inject into; the estimator's stated sensitivity is therefore the synthetic ladders of the receipt, as the protocol prescribes: an injected passive relic is recovered at 10 % of block mass in 9 of 9 white-noise runs, at 5 % in 8 of 9 Poisson-deposited spheres (10 % in the ninth), and at 10 % in 7 of 9 shared-drive runs (20 % in two) |
| F4 robustness | pass, vacuous | no run is measurable under both the main configuration and a variant (two variants admit runs of their own, six and three, reported below), so there is nothing to compare |
| F5 support | **fail** | 0 measurable conditions of the 3 required; 179 of 1,320 run-epochs eligible |

This is an instrument result. It says nothing about nested resonance memory, in either direction. What it retires is the plan in [`docs/halo/NEXT_MEMORY_ESTIMATOR.md`](../docs/halo/NEXT_MEMORY_ESTIMATOR.md) to qualify the estimator on the saved meshes before commissioning another grid: the saved meshes cannot qualify it, and the reasons are properties of the grid, listed below, that a new grid can be designed around.

## Three facts about the grid

**1. The seed survives the dynamics in 3 of 20 conditions.** The three seeds of a condition are independent draws of the initial particle positions; the protocol's remark that seeds share positions means that each seed's draw repeats across the self-gravity and gain/loss cells. The dynamics erase the draw. In the shipped-defaults family the three seeds of a condition are copies of one another at every self-gravity (raw correlation 0.99999 or 1.00000). Gain/loss 0.5 makes near-copies of every condition in both families (0.970 or higher; 0.9957 or higher at self-gravity 0.3 and above). Spinning Chladni at self-gravity 0 and 0.15 is also near-copy (0.970 to 0.9998). Only Spinning Chladni at gain/loss 0 and self-gravity 0.3, 0.5 and 0.8 keeps three distinct seeds (0.763, 0.224, 0.077). In the drive-locked conditions the seed-to-seed difference of the compressed templates is the Poisson shot noise of a 4,194,304-particle deposit (0.9 to 1.1 times the expected shot variance in Spinning Chladni at self-gravity 0), while the angular structure the seeds share is some thousands of times that variance in the wall shell and 5 to 10 times it in the clamped core. E3 there is a particle-count effect in the wrong direction: more particles make the copies more exact. Two counts of "copy" are used here and they differ: by the raw field (correlation with the most similar stranger 0.97 or higher) 17 of 20 conditions are copies; by the templates E3 actually compares (median largest template correlation at or above 0.9) 14 of 20 are. The difference is the twin pairs of the two static conditions below: their raw fields correlate at 0.99999, yet their compressed templates pass E3 in 17 and 20 of 22 epochs (median template correlation 0.856 and 0.651), because the angular residual E3 compares is about 0.03 % of the block variance and is dominated by shot noise. E3 exists so that a run is never compared with a copy of its own relic under another seed's name; on this grid that is the usual case, and E3 cannot see a twin whose only difference is shot noise.

**2. Where the seeds differ, the matter collapses, and the compressed relic of a collapsed core is a handful of cells in about half of the collapse epochs.** The three seed-diverse conditions hold 41 eligible run-epochs of 198, and E2 refuses 33, 34 and 48 of their 66. The wall shell itself is not what E2 refuses: in Spinning Chladni at self-gravity 0 and 0.15 the current block and all three relics pass E1 and E2 together in 20, 16, 15 and 9 of 22 epochs per run (gain/loss 0 and 0.5 in turn), and it is E3 that refuses those conditions. Of the 609 predicted-field refusals, 380 are collapse epochs, where the inner block holds at least 90 % of the mass, 104 are the empty-block defaults conditions at self-gravity 0, whose relic is about 2.4 cells, and 125 lie between; the failing relics have a median of 4.9 effective cells (273 of the 609 in [4, 7], 210 below 4). In the 691 collapse run-epochs of the grid, a shrink by two sends the core below 8 effective cells in 328, about half (65 % once the 12 static runs are excluded), typically to 4 to 7, which is what the one-cell control arm of the [2026-09-02 audit](2026-09-02_cross_epoch_memory_preregistered.md) looked like and what the gate was written to refuse. In Spinning Chladni at self-gravity 0.5 the mass inside the inner block swings between 0.007 and 0.998 from one epoch to the next (epochs 14 to 15: 0.007 to 0.548; 16 to 17: 0.049 to 0.992; 18 to 19: 0.998 to 0.120), so the relic footprint alternates between wide and compact and eligibility comes in isolated epochs (8, 8 and 7 of 22 for its three seeds). A run needs 12.

**3. Twelve runs are static, and the only two conditions with enough eligible epochs are among them.** In the defaults family at gain/loss 0 and self-gravity 0.15 to 0.8, every run's full density correlates with its own previous epoch at 0.99995 to 0.99999 (median over 23 epoch pairs; the lowest single pair is 0.9976). These fields barely change over the run's 230 simulated seconds (23 epoch intervals of 10 s): 99 % of the mass sits in about 100 cells of 32,768 (medians 101 to 103), and the picture is the same 24 times to within a correlation of 0.99995 and within 0.03 % of the block variance; the angular residual the estimator scores does evolve in the twin runs (median epoch-to-epoch correlation 0.78 to 0.98), and does not in the odd seeds (0.9999 and 1.0000). Every one of them sits at exactly one of two lattice sites, with its centroid at (15.5, 15.5, 15.5) or at (15.5, 15.5, 14.5), one cell lower along z. The two conditions with at least 12 eligible epochs per run, default sg0.3 gl0 (19, 17, 17) and default sg0.5 gl0 (15, 15, 15), are static conditions in which three seeds fell on two sites: a duplicate pair on one site and a singleton one cell away. Rolling the odd seed's mesh by one cell along z makes it correlate with the pair at 0.999 or better. In sg0.3, seed 777's angular residual carries 43 to 48 % of its block variance at every epoch, against 0.03 % for seeds 12345 and 31337, whose compressed relics correlate with each other at 0.66 to 0.97; in sg0.5 the odd seed is 12345 (44 to 48 % against about 0.008 %). Averaged over all 22 scored epochs, the odd seed's own-relic correlation is +0.641 against +0.089 for a stranger's relic (sg0.3) and +0.646 against +0.056 (sg0.5). That is the persistent-asymmetry confound named in protocol §1: a fixed angular pattern that persists in the lab frame is predicted by its own compressed relic and by nobody else's, and the synthetic persistent-dipole control shows the estimator detects it. The seed-balance gate E5b refuses all six runs because two seeds are copies and one is different, which collapses the 3 × 3 run-by-relic matrix to a single informative row: the seed null values are (+0.005, +0.683, +0.635) for seed 777 in sg0.3, (+0.0045, +0.695, +0.655) for its two siblings, whose eligible epochs differ, and (+0.269, +0.003, +0.315) for all three runs of sg0.5, against a rule that refuses a largest value of at least 0.05 exceeding three times the smallest.

## What the variants report

The protocol fixes eleven variants of the free choices for its robustness falsifier and says that measurability changes under them are reported, not scored. Nine of the eleven leave 0 runs measurable. Two admit runs:

- **E2 threshold 4 instead of 8** admits six runs. Here S is the estimator's score for a run: its correlation with its own compressed relic minus the null predicted from the strangers' entries, averaged over the run's eligible epochs (protocol §7). Four are not detected (the three seeds of default sg0.8 gl0 at S = −0.028, +0.046 and +0.088; Spinning Chladni sg0.5 gl0 seed 777 at S = −0.014). Two are: Spinning Chladni sg0.8 gl0 seeds 12345 and 31337, each with 12 eligible epochs, S = +0.172 and +0.153, p = 0.0120 for both, with the observed S at the top of its relabelling distribution (the nominal floor of a four-block test is 0.0123), mean own-relic correlation +0.172 and +0.175 against +0.010 and −0.017 for a stranger's, block-bootstrap intervals [+0.126, +0.282] and [+0.066, +0.267]. Under the main configuration these two runs have one eligible epoch each. The condition's third seed, 777, reaches 15 eligible epochs under this variant with the largest S of the three (+0.199) and is refused by the seed-balance gate (null values 0.026, 0.076, 0.092), so the condition is not measurable under the variant either. Of the eleven epochs the variant adds, the detected run's own compressed relic has fewer than 8 effective cells in ten (4.1 to 7.99), and each run's S is carried by four epochs whose own templates have 4.6, 4.9, 4.1 and 7.1 effective cells (seed 12345) and 5.2, 6.2, 6.7 and 10.4 (seed 31337). Their unmapped contrast (+0.231 and +0.189) is at least as large as the mapped one, the signature protocol §9 assigns to a persistent asymmetry rather than a compressed relic. Even under this variant only one condition is fully measurable and it has no detection, so F5 fails there too.
- **Hand-over scale 0.10 instead of 0.05** admits the three runs of default sg0.5 gl0 and detects seed 12345: S = +0.647, p = 0.0035, own-relic correlation +0.645 against +0.062 for a stranger's over its 15 eligible epochs, bootstrap interval [+0.642, +0.650]; the other two read S = +0.302 (p = 0.112) and +0.230 (p = 0.235). Nothing about the epochs changes; the variant moves the seed null values from (+0.269, +0.003, +0.315) to (+0.390, −0.0015, +0.429), the odd seed's null crosses zero, and the protocol's mixed-sign clause switches the seed-balance gate off. That null is +0.0031 ± 0.0030 under the main configuration and −0.0015 ± 0.0031 under the variant: zero within one standard error both ways, so whether these three runs are measurable rests on the sign of a number that is not distinguishable from zero. The detected run is the static odd seed of the third fact: its angular residual in the inner block barely changes between consecutive epochs (median correlation 0.99995, 19 of 23 pairs at 0.9995 or better), its participation ratio stays between 14.2 and 14.6, and it correlates with its own compressed image because it does not move.

Under the frozen protocol these numbers decide nothing. E2 is 8 because the old control arm's relic had a participation ratio near 1 and the synthetic controls put real compressed relics at 13 to 22 effective cells, so 8 is the gate between them; the hand-over scale sets how the null prediction blends its two forms and, in the script, the 0.05 floors of the two run-level gates as well, neither of which matters for these three runs. Both detections rest on fields the main configuration refuses on purpose.

## What a measurable grid needs

The three facts above are three design constraints, and each is checkable before a confirmatory grid is run:

1. **A regime whose dynamics do not erase the seed.** Only Spinning Chladni at gain/loss 0 and self-gravity 0.3 to 0.8 kept three seeds apart for 24 epochs, and that window is the only one whose eligibility is limited by the dynamics rather than by shot noise: 1 to 8 eligible epochs of 22 per run at the recorded count (6, 6, 3 at self-gravity 0.3; 8, 8, 7 at 0.5; 1, 1, 1 at 0.8) and 3 to 11 at self-gravity 0.3 and 0.5, never more than 3 at 0.8, at the six resampled counts recorded in the diagnostics file. More particles do not help; in every drive-locked condition they make the seeds more exactly alike.
2. **A relic footprint of at least 8 effective cells at every scored epoch.** A shrink by two compresses a collapsed core into 4 to 7 cells. Settings that do not collapse, a smaller shrink, or scoring only epochs where the relic is extended are the candidate fixes; the last two change the estimand and have to be written down first.
3. **No static runs and no odd seed.** A field that does not change between epochs turns the epochs into one observation, and a condition in which two seeds are copies and one is different cannot be told apart from a single run with memory. In the defaults preset this is a two-site lattice attractor, so a fourth or fifth seed would only add duplicates.

Fewer particles is the one lever that can be tested on the recorded meshes without new runs, by Poisson-resampling them, which assumes the collapsed and shell states themselves do not change with particle count; the pilot is the test of that assumption. The diagnostics file records one draw at each of six counts from 3,000,000 down to 16,384 particles. At 1,048,576 exactly one condition is fully measurable (default sg0.15 gl0, 20 of 22 epochs eligible per run, none of the three detected), and the static displaced seed of default sg0.3 gl0 becomes measurable there and is detected, which is the confound of the third fact and not memory. At the other five counts no run is measurable. No count reaches the three conditions F5 needs.

The next step is a pilot, not a grid: Spinning Chladni at gain/loss 0 and self-gravity 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5, three seeds, longer than 24 epochs, with default sg0.15 gl0 at about one million particles as the measurability control. It has worked if all three runs of each of three conditions reach 12 eligible epochs and the seed null values are either all below 0.05 or within a factor of three, so that at least nine runs are measurable and F3 becomes evaluable. Only then is a confirmatory grid worth registering. The estimator itself is unchanged: it still has to pass F3 and F4 on a grid that can support it, and four of its own rules should be reconsidered in the next protocol before that: the seed-balance gate has no tolerance for a null value within one standard error of zero; a field that does not move between epochs is refused by that gate for the wrong stated reason rather than by a rule that names it; E3 cannot see a twin whose only difference is shot noise, so a raw-field twin check belongs beside it; and the protocol's prose puts "not qualified" ahead of "insufficient support" while the script tests support first, a difference that did not matter here because no falsifier failed.
