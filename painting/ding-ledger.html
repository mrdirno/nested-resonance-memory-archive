The Ding Ledger

You’re the last trade through, so from final coat to turnover your finish is the one everybody else’s work lands on — and without a date on each mark, every scuff in the building reads as a painting punch item. This is the ledger that ends that: each ding as a row, the day you found it, what was in the room when you did, the fix it actually takes — because a touch-up that flashes is a whole wall — and the hours as counts. Group it by who was in the room and the tallies write your PM’s back-charge memo for him.

Observed, not accused — and counts, not costs. “Their scaffold was in it” is a fact; “they did it” is an argument this page won’t make for you, and Unknown — found it is an honest answer. There are no dollars on here: hours and material ride as counts, and your office owns the pricing. It feeds your PM’s back-charge and shortens the GC’s punch — it is not itself a back-charge, a punch list, or anybody’s numbered record.

The job — typed once, rides on every copy
