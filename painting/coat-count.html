Coat Count

Every coat as a row: where, which surface, which coat, what went on it — product and color as your schedule prints them — the batch number off the fives, the gallons drawn, and what your own meter read while it went on. The empties with the batch numbers leave in the dumpster tonight; this is what’s still here in March when a wall flashes and somebody wants matching touch-up. Copy it for a spreadsheet and it pastes straight into the office’s sheet.

It counts coats — it never grades them. There is no “done”, “ready” or “approved” on this page. The conditions on a row are what your instruments read, beside nothing — the page sets no limit and does no math on them. Product, color and sheen ride exactly as your schedule and your cans print them; this page picks none of them and holds no formula.

The job — typed once, rides on every copy

Each coat

Name the area once — it becomes a button and it carries. The surface, the coat, the product and the batch carry to the next row, because a crew runs one product across a floor of rooms — change them only when they change.

The building so far

Group by

Tap a row when a later coat goes over it — it moves to Recoated: that film is under another one now, and the visible coat is a different row. Blank means it’s still the coat you’d touch up to. The pencil opens a row to fix a number, add a note, or delete it.

Copy it — the record that outlives the empties

Include

Everything is the whole diary — every coat, in order, for your file. The visible coats is what’s on top right now: the touch-up map, with the product and batch beside each area. The spreadsheet copy is every row with a tab between the columns, and it’s also your backup — this lives in this browser on this phone, so copy it somewhere the day it matters.
