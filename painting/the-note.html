Painting · the door everybody thinks is a suggestion

Wet Area Notice

You closed the room because there is wet paint in it, and to everybody walking past, that tape is a suggestion. So write it down once: what you closed and which rooms, the time it went closed, and when it can be touched and when it can be worked in — in your own words, off your own data sheet. Then the asks an occupied day actually needs: the air handler off while you’re in it and back on when they say, the intakes sealed, the floor cleared, nobody through that door till you say. It states no dry time, no recoat time and no product, and it never says the air is clear. It’s what you closed, written down, so a hand on a wet frame at six is a conversation you already had.

What you send

It’s a notice, not a permit and not a clearance. What’s on it is what you closed and when you say your finish can be touched and worked in, copied off your own data sheet — this page states no dry time, no recoat time, no cure time, no product, no temperature and no humidity, and it never will; the manufacturer’s times are the manufacturer’s to state. It says nothing about the air. When a space can be breathed, what the ventilation has to do and what anybody has to wear belong to your own safety plan, the SDS and the building — no line here is an exposure call, a clearance or an occupancy release, and “touchable” and “workable” are statements about your finish, nothing else. The air handler, the intakes, the alarm and the tenants are whoever runs them’s; every line below that mentions one is a question aimed at its owner. Getting in the door, the lift and where paint sleeps between shifts are Getting In; the write-up you dictate later is Write-Up Setup.

✦ Something the building always asks you the day you close a room that isn’t on the list? Wish for it — it gets added, and you get the credit.
