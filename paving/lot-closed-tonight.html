Lot Closed Tonight

On an occupied lot your cones decide where two hundred people park tonight, and the one thing you can never be is surprised at seven by a car sitting on wet seal. So write down what you’re closing and which sections, the night it’s closed, when cars can come back in your own words off your own spec sheet, where the cones and tape go and what stays open — and the ask: the building tells the tenants, a name who moves a car, the fire lane is theirs to name, nobody pulls a cone before you say. It states no cure time and no product. It’s what you’re closing, written down so you both know.

What you send

It’s a notice, not a permit and not a traffic plan. What’s on it is what you are closing and when cars come back in your words, copied off your own spec sheet — this page states no cure time, no open-to-traffic time, no product and no rate, and it never will; the manufacturer’s time is the manufacturer’s to state. The fire lane is the fire marshal’s to name and nothing here marks one. The tow list, the tenant notice and the plan and the permit for anything on the street are the building’s to hold, and nothing here is shaped like any of them.

✦ Something the building always asks you the night you close a lot that isn’t on the list? Wish for it — it gets added, and you get the credit.
