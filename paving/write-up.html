The Write-Up Setup

The base you were handed, with your string line off the curb saying one thing and the sheet saying another, and the spot by the dock that pumped under the truck. What was under your mat the day it rolled — the sleeves and lids you were told about against the ones you found. The day you couldn’t pave, with eighteen loads ordered and a crew standing, and who called it. The layout that didn’t fit the lot, section by section, and who decided which one went. The car that drove across fresh seal at seven with the cone already moved. The paint tracked into the lobby and who walked it with you. The lot you handed back and what you told them, in your own words, about keeping off it — and whatever else you have to write that isn’t on the list. Pick the one you’re stuck with and this hands you the exact instructions to paste into your AI — once. After that it’s: dump the mess at the tailgate, get back something you can send without touching it. It never states a mix temperature, a density, a stall count, a cure time or why a mat failed, and it never calls a lot open or accepted — it writes down what you saw, what your string line read, what the ticket said and what you did.

Nothing you type here leaves your phone — it stays in this browser. What you paste into your AI afterwards is your call, so keep customer detail and anyone’s personal information out of it. This builds instructions, not certified data: it never supplies a mix design, a lay-down temperature, a density or a thickness — the lab and the plant own those; never states a stall count, a stall or aisle dimension, a slope or a fire-lane length — the sheet, the civil and the fire marshal own those; never names a cure time, an open-to-traffic time or a product rate — that’s the manufacturer’s sheet and your own spec sheet, in your words; never turns a load count into a yield or a proof-roll into a verdict; and it never states that a lot is open, complete, accepted or warrantable. Check anything feeding a purchase, a pay application or a safety decision against your own contract, the sheet in your hand, the ticket in your pocket, and the people certified to make those calls.
