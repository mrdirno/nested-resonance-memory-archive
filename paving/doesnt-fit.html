Doesn’t Fit

The striping sheet was drawn on a lot that didn’t have a pole base in it yet, or a hydrant, or a curb return that came in a foot long. You find out with a chalk box and a wheel, the afternoon before paint. Put a row on every place the sheet and the lot disagree — the spot in your words, what the sheet draws there, quoted, what your tape found, what’s in the way, and what you need decided — and send it to the man who can decide it while the layout is still chalk. After paint it’s a grind-out, and it’s an argument you already lost.

The sheet is quoted and the tape is yours. Every row says what the sheet draws at a spot, copied off the sheet and rev named at the top, and what your tape found there, in your words. This page carries no stall count, no stall or aisle dimension, no slope, no sign height and no accessible-stall call of its own — the sheet and the people who stamp it own every one of those, and this page asks them which one goes. It’s an ask for a decision before paint: not an RFI, not a change order, and it doesn’t authorize anybody to do extra work. Double-check it before you send it, because a row you forgot is a stall you paint twice.

The job — typed once, rides on every copy

Name the sheet and its rev. It is the difference between a list the civil can answer against his own set by tonight and one striper’s opinion he has to re-walk with you — and if he is holding a later rev than you are, that is its own answer and you want it before the paint truck is loaded.

Each spot

Name the spot once — it becomes a button and it carries. The section and the sheet carry to the next row too, because you lay out a section at a time off one sheet. Leave anything blank you didn’t measure: an empty field is honest and a guessed one is not.
