Under the Mat

Four kits on this rack wrote you a letter about what they’ve got under your base — the landscaper’s sleeves, the gate loop, the pole bases, the lids and the monuments — and none of them had anywhere to hear back from. This is the letter back. Walk the section before the base rolls and put a row on everything that’s under it: whose it is, what it is, who told you and off which list, whether you watched it go in or were only told, and whether the iron’s to grade. Send it to each outfit at the gate they already know. After the mat it’s a saw cut, and everybody knows whose day that costs.

What you were told and what you saw, not a drawing. Every row is something somebody else has under your base, in your words, off the crossing lists and the civil named at the top, with what your own eyes found beside it. This page supplies no cover depth, no separation, no sleeve or pipe size and no spec — their plans, their submittals and whoever stamps them own all of that. It’s an ask to close out what’s under your mat before you roll: not an RFI, not a change order, and it doesn’t authorize anybody to do extra work. Double-check it before you send it, because a sleeve nobody walked is a bore somebody pays for.

The job — typed once, rides on every copy

Name the lists and the sheet. A sleeve counted against the landscaper’s own crossing list is a fact he can confirm from his truck this afternoon; the same sleeve described from memory is a phone call nobody can find the day the saw comes out. And whatever somebody told you at the tailgate, put it in a row here with their name on it.

Each thing under it

Name the spot once — it becomes a button and it carries. The section and the gate carry to the next row too, because you walk a section at a time. Leave anything blank you didn’t see: an empty field is honest and a guessed one is not.

The walk so far

Group by

Tap a row as it moves: blank is walked, then Sent when it went to whoever owns it, then Confirmed when they came back on it, then In — I saw it when you watched it go in and get capped. The pencil opens a row to fix a line, add a note, or delete it.

Copy it — the block that goes to each outfit at its gate

Include

The spreadsheet copy is every row with a tab between the columns — it is what the super wants beside his own crossing schedule, and it is also your backup: this lives in this browser on this phone, so copy it somewhere the day it matters.
