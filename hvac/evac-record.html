Evac Record

Pump on. Valved off at your number. Then a reading whenever you look at the gauge. You tap — the clock writes the time, and it keeps counting while the phone is in your pocket. At the end you copy the times and the numbers into a message instead of reconstructing them at the shop two hours later.

No target on this page, and no verdict — on purpose. This tool does not know your shop’s number, the equipment manufacturer’s number, or anybody’s spec, it carries no table of them, and it will never tell you a system passed. It writes down what you read off your gauge and when. What those readings mean is your call.

Mark it — you never type a time

Started before you opened this? Tap the mark anyway, then hit the pencil on that row and put the real time in. It gets labelled time typed in from then on, on the page and in what you send — which is the honest thing, because every other stamp on the record is the clock’s word and that one is yours.

The record

Tap the pencil to fix a time, fix a reading, add a note, or delete a mark you fat-fingered. Delete the valved off mark and the button comes back.

What it’s on — fill this when you send it, not at the pump

The unit tag is the first thing on the message, because that is what somebody searches their mail for nine months from now when a compressor comes back.

Send it

Start the next one clears the marks and keeps your name, your gauge and the job — three units on one roof is one afternoon, not three setups. Copy first. This record lives in this browser on this phone and nowhere else: a browser you have not opened in a couple of weeks can clear it out, and a new phone definitely will.
