Not Ready To Plant

Three kits on this rack built you a chip and none of them could write you a line — this is the answer back. Walk the beds before the plant lands and name what stops the planting, in your words: the grade that’s been driven on all winter, the base rock and the busted block in the top foot, the topsoil that never came, the water that isn’t on, the sleeves nobody put in. Then send the only ask that matters — fix it and tell me when, or tell me in writing to plant it as it sits. Because a plant that goes into ground that wasn’t ready dies on your warranty, and you are the one digging it out in July explaining it.

What you send

It’s a condition list, not a verdict. Nothing on it says anybody’s work failed anything — it says what you found, where, in your words, with anything you measured taken off your own level and set beside what your own plan sheet says. It states no slope, no grade requirement, no soil spec, no compaction number and no rate of anything, it never says a bed is ready, and it doesn’t stop the job — it hands whoever runs the job the choice, on the record: clear it, or direct it planted as it sits.

✦ A hold your mornings hit that isn’t on the list? Wish for it — it gets added, and you get the credit.
