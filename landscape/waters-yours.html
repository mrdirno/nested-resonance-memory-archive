Landscape · the day the water becomes somebody else’s

Water’s Yours

Everything you planted lives or dies on what they do with the water after you leave, and the day it’s in and running is the day they own it. So write down who has the controller and where it is, what you set the clock to in your own words copied off the face, what’s running and what isn’t — and the ask: nobody kills the water or changes the clock without telling you, the day it goes off for a freeze, who waters the days you’re not there, and the date the maintenance clock starts off their contract. It recommends nothing. It’s what you set, written down so you both know.

What you send

It’s a handback, not a schedule. What’s on it is what you set the clock to, copied off the face in your words — this page recommends no run time, no rate, no schedule and no seasonal adjustment, and it never will. The controller code is never on it: give it by phone. The water bill, the restrictions and the backflow test are theirs to run — the tester files that with the water purveyor, and nothing here is shaped like his form.

✦ Something the building always asks you at turnover that isn’t on the list? Wish for it — it gets added, and you get the credit.
