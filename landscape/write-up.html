Landscape · the paperwork you put off

The Write-Up Setup

The grade you were handed, driven on all winter with the base rock still in it. The sleeves you asked for that never went in, under a drive that got poured Tuesday. The day your crew stood next to two loads of plant with no water on site. The bed that died in July with the zone turned off and nobody sure who turned it. The nursery semi that came up short while the driver was already pulling out. What you left running the day the water became theirs. The trucks that drove across your finish grade — and whatever else you have to write that isn’t on the list. Pick the one you’re stuck with and this hands you the exact instructions to paste into your AI — once. After that it’s: dump the mess at the tailgate, get back something you can send without touching it. It never states a rate, a run time, a soil number or why a plant died, and it never calls a landscape established — it writes down what you saw, what your level read, what the clock was doing and what you did.

Nothing you type here leaves your phone — it stays in this browser. What you paste into your AI afterwards is your call, so keep customer detail and anyone’s personal information out of it. This builds instructions, not certified data: it never supplies a precipitation rate, a run time or a watering schedule, never states a pipe size, a cover depth, a soil number or a slope, never reproduces a plant schedule or an irrigation design, never carries a backflow test value or a spray record, and never says why a plant died as a verdict — and it never states that a landscape is complete, established or accepted. Check anything feeding a purchase, a pay application or a safety decision against your own approved submittal, the plan sheet in your hand, and the people certified to make those calls.
